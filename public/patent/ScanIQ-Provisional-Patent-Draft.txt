TITLE

ADDITIVE MANUFACTURING OF TOPOLOGICAL CHIRAL METAMATERIAL RESONATORS
FOR NEAR-FIELD TERAHERTZ SPECTROSCOPY AND QUANTUM-ASSISTED
DEVELOPMENTAL PHENOTYPING

FIELD

The disclosure relates to additive micromanufacturing, topological metamaterials,
and non-invasive biomedical metrology: systems and methods for manufacturing
functionally graded, chiral silk-nanofiber topological resonators using dynamic
field-assisted extrusion, and using those resonators in opposed near-field
terahertz polarimetry, broadband impedance spectroscopy, and nitrogen-vacancy
diamond quantum magnetometry to assess biological developmental state without
biopsy.

BACKGROUND

Embryo assessment in cattle and horse production still infers competence from
morphology, timing, or occasional invasive genetic assays. A recipient cow or
mare is occupied whether the transferred embryo is alive or already stalled.
Electrical impedance spectroscopy already shows that developmental state moves
a measurable spectrum (bovine 1.49 MHz differential real-impedance marker;
medaka multi-stage EIS; porcine oocyte phenotyping to 30 MHz). Conventional
electrodes and bulk THz transmission through water are inadequate. Prior art
exists separately for NV microfluidic magnetometers, resonant biosensors,
magnetic-field-assisted printing, silk THz metamaterials, and Möbius resonators.
The invention is the closed loop: field-native co-design, manufactured topological
resonators, differential multimodal sensing, temporal mapping to developmental
trajectories, and measured-response redesign.

SUMMARY

A platform integrating (i) SDF + spatial material tensor design, (ii) field-aligned
biomimetic nanocomposites, (iii) Möbius or other non-orientable resonators,
(iv) evanescent RF/THz polarimetry and broadband impedance, (v) optional
differential NV-diamond magnetometry with environmental cancellation, and
(vi) a temporal multimodal model outputting stage, trajectory deviation, and
competence probability.

Q is defined for the combined resonator-sample system. Schumann resonances
are environmental baselines, not biological clocks. Optimization maximizes
developmental-state information per unit exposure, not Q alone. Stimulation /
growth-enhancement is a later, separate research branch.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION (compressed for this text export; see on-screen specification)

A. Processor maintains f(x) (SDF) and M(x) = {fiber fraction, director, chirality,
permittivity, conductivity}. Gradient-informed slicing drives toolpaths, coil
currents, and cure dose. As-built metrology writes back into the field.

B. Feedstock: methacrylated silk fibroin + aramid nanofibers + photoinitiator.
Micro-aperture 50–250 μm. Rotating B and/or E field programs fiber director.
UV/blue cure locks anisotropy. Novelty is voxel-wise chirality and resonant-
response targeting from the implicit field, not magnetic printing per se.

C. Möbius ribbon (odd multiple of 180° twist) yields Berry phase π and fractional
half-integer modes. Opposed copies about a 100–170 μm culture channel.

D. Evanescent interrogation (0.1–3 THz and kHz–tens of MHz EIS). LCP/RCP
modulation. Measure Δf, ΔQ, S-parameters, phase, Stokes [S0..S3], ΔS3.

E. Optional NV ODMR gradiometer: sensing node near the well, reference node
offset. Cancel geomagnetic, 50/60 Hz, Schumann (~7.83 Hz). Near-field only.

F. AI discriminator ingests multimodal time series against separate meiotic and
mitotic atlases. Outputs stage, Mahalanobis deviation, arrest risk, uncertainty.
Discovery models walled from clinical claims.

G. Closed-loop redesign of M(x) from measured transfer function, including
embodiments without a biological specimen.

H. Sensing first. No assumption that EM exposure improves viability. Human
embryo work requires reproductive-research oversight.

CLAIMS

1. A resonant biofabrication and developmental sensing system, comprising: an
additive manufacturing apparatus comprising an extrusion aperture, an
electromagnetic orientation assembly configured to apply a dynamically rotating
vector field within an extrusion print zone, and an irradiation source configured
to initiate in-situ photopolymerization of an extrudable composite feedstock
containing anisotropic microstructures to fabricate a solid topological resonator;
a sensing fixture comprising at least one manufactured topological resonator
positioned adjacent to a biological incubation chamber; an electromagnetic
source operably coupled to pump modulated high-frequency signals through the
topological resonator to establish an evanescent near-field within the biological
incubation chamber; and a detection subsystem configured to measure
perturbations in a quality factor (Q-factor), an eigenmode resonance frequency,
and a polarization state of the topological resonator induced by a biological
specimen positioned within the biological incubation chamber.

2. The system of claim 1, wherein the topological resonator comprises a Möbius
strip geometry having a single-sided non-orientable surface and an accumulated
geometric Pancharatnam-Berry phase of π, supporting fractional half-integer
electromagnetic modes.

3. The system of claim 1, wherein the extrudable composite feedstock comprises
regenerated silk fibroin, aramid nanofibers (ANFs), and a photoinitiator, wherein
the cured topological resonator exhibits a flexural strength of at least 450 MPa.

4. The system of claim 1, wherein the electromagnetic orientation assembly
comprises a multi-pole stator array that generates a rotating magnetic vector
field at a frequency between 0.1 Hz and 100 Hz, orienting the anisotropic
microstructures along a three-dimensional vector field defined by an implicit
signed distance function.

5. The system of claim 1, wherein the electromagnetic source comprises a pulsed
terahertz emitter operating within a frequency range of 0.1 THz to 3.0 THz, and
wherein the detection subsystem comprises a polarimetric electro-optic sampling
detector configured to measure time-resolved Stokes parameters [S0, S1, S2, S3].

6. The system of claim 1, further comprising a quantum magnetometry subsystem,
the subsystem comprising: a primary diamond substrate comprising an ensemble
of nitrogen-vacancy (NV) color centers positioned adjacent to the biological
incubation chamber; a secondary reference diamond substrate comprising an
ensemble of NV color centers spaced apart from the biological incubation
chamber; and a differential processing unit configured to perform optically
detected magnetic resonance (ODMR) vector magnetometry and execute adaptive
common-mode cancellation of environmental baseline electromagnetic fields.

7. The system of claim 6, wherein the environmental baseline electromagnetic
fields canceled by the differential processing unit comprise Earth-ionosphere
Schumann resonances having a fundamental frequency of approximately 7.83 Hz.

8. The system of claim 1, further comprising an artificial intelligence
discriminator stored in a non-transitory memory and executed by a processor,
configured to: ingest time-series streams of the measured Q-factor, resonance
frequency, polarization state, wideband electrical impedance spectra, and optical
time-lapse morphokinetics from the biological specimen; and map the time-series
streams against a calibrated developmental trajectory to output a predictive score
of biological viability.

9. The system of claim 8, wherein the wideband electrical impedance spectra
include differential real impedance measurements sampled across a frequency
band encompassing 1.49 MHz.

10. A method for non-invasively assessing biological developmental viability,
comprising: positioning an embryonic specimen within a microfluidic sensing well
positioned between an opposed pair of 3D-printed topological Möbius resonators;
coupling polarization-modulated terahertz pulses into the opposed topological
Möbius resonators to generate an evanescent electromagnetic field that interacts
with the embryonic specimen; measuring shifts in a complex resonance frequency
(Δfr), a quality factor (ΔQ), and a terahertz circular dichroism (ΔS3) parameter
across a developmental monitoring period; measuring localized vector magnetic
field transients using a differential nitrogen-vacancy diamond quantum
magnetometer, canceling ambient Schumann resonance baselines; and processing
the measured resonance shifts, quality factor, circular dichroism, vector magnetic
field transients, and synchronized optical time-lapse frames through a temporal
transformer model to calculate a developmental trajectory deviation metric.

11. The method of claim 10, wherein the opposed pair of topological Möbius
resonators are additive-manufactured from a synthetic silk fibroin and aramid
nanofiber composite whose internal molecular director orientation is steered via
a rotating electromagnetic field during micro-extrusion.

12. The method of claim 10, wherein the temporal transformer model maintains
separate, decoupled latent training representations for gamete meiotic maturation
and embryonic mitotic cleavage.

13. A non-transitory computer-readable storage medium storing instructions that,
when executed by one or more processors, cause the one or more processors to:
compile an implicit signed distance field (SDF) and a spatial material orientation
field representing a topological Möbius resonator; translate the SDF and spatial
material orientation field into synchronized machine toolpaths for an additive
manufacturing apparatus, controlling vertical printhead translation, in-situ
photopolymerization dosage, and dynamic multi-axis coil currents in an

common-mode environmental noise from the quantum magnetometry signals

ABSTRACT

Systems and methods manufacture a topologically chiral resonator from a
field-native geometry and spatial material orientation, using a roto-field aperture
printhead and in-situ polymerization of a hierarchical silk or polymer composite.
The resonator is positioned adjacent to a biological specimen and interrogated by
evanescent RF/THz and impedance measurements, optionally with differential
NV-diamond magnetometry and environmental cancellation. A temporal multimodal
model maps spectral perturbations to a developmental-state trajectory and
viability estimate. Measured electromagnetic response can update the material
field for subsequent fabrication.
