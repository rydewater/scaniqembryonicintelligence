2. Your aperture-printhead idea has real manufacturing precedents

There are actually three viable fabrication families.

A. Silk-fibroin direct ink writing

Silk fibroin has already been extruded through fine nozzles. Direct-write silk structures with fibers around 5 µm diameter were demonstrated years ago, and newer systems use highly concentrated silk fibroin with shear-thinning behavior. 

More recent silk inks use pre-photocrosslinking + enzymatic crosslinking, while nozzle shear promotes β-sheet organization and stabilizes the printed structure. 

That is quite close to what you're calling an aperture printhead polymerizing process.

B. Magnetic-field-assisted printing

This is where your rotating-field concept gets especially strong.

Magnetically assisted DIW already exists. In a well-known system, anisotropic platelets were oriented using a rotating magnetic field of about 40 mT at 8.3 Hz during multimaterial printing. 

Modern reviews describe magnetic alignment occurring either inside/near the extrusion nozzle or after deposition before curing, allowing programmed anisotropy. 

A 2026 study even integrated field control into stereolithography to align Fe3O4-decorated carbon nanofibers before photopolymerization. 

So I would build our hypothetical printhead around:

annular/coaxial aperture → extrusion → rotating-field orientation zone → optical/chemical curing zone.

The interesting patent territory would not be simply applying a magnetic field during printing — that already has prior art.

The potential novelty is:

using ScanIQ's SDF/voxel field representation to calculate the desired local anisotropic orientation, chirality and resonant response voxel-by-voxel, then dynamically rotate the field while depositing and polymerizing the material.

That's a different proposition.

4. There is already measurable embryonic spectral data

This is probably the most important result I found for the patent.

We should not claim that particular embryo stages already have established intrinsic electromagnetic resonant frequencies. I couldn't substantiate that.

But something much more defensible already exists:

Bovine embryos

A 2024 IEEE study built a microfluidic system specifically for non-invasive differential impedance spectroscopy of bovine embryos.

It observed a decrease in the real differential impedance at 1.49 MHz correlated with increasing embryonic cell mass. 

That's a hard quantitative anchor.

Medaka developmental atlas

Researchers modeled 13 developmental stages from zygote through hatching using differential electrical impedance spectroscopy.

They extracted:

relaxation frequency,

real impedance,

imaginary impedance,

relaxation-model parameters.


Those characteristics changed with development. 

In subsequent work, membrane capacitance increased while equivalent membrane resistance generally decreased from neurula through hatching, consistent with changing membrane permeability and thickness. 

And a 2025 follow-on study reported that differential EIS distinguished living versus dead embryos, with lower-frequency response separating viability and higher-frequency response reflecting degree of inactivation. 

That is extraordinarily relevant to our patent.

We're no longer proposing:

> "Maybe embryos have measurable EM differences."



We're saying:

> Developmentally dependent electrical spectral differences have already been demonstrated.



Our invention would extend how they're measured and interpreted.

7. Important correction concerning Earth's resonance

I think when you said Earth's human resonance, you meant the Schumann resonance.

The fundamental is approximately 7.83 Hz, followed by modes near 14.3, 20.8, 27.3 and 33.8 Hz.
